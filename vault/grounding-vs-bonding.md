# Grounding vs Bonding

Electrical safety under Title 24, Part 3 relies on distinguishing grounding from bonding.

---

## 1. Grounding (Connection to Earth)

- **Definition Authority:** NEC Article 100; CEC Article 100.
- **Governing Article:** NEC Article 250, Part III.
- **Physical Objective:** Connects electrical systems and circuit conductors to earth to limit voltages imposed by lightning, line surges, or unintentional contact with higher-voltage lines.
- **Grounding Electrode Conductor (GEC):** Sized strictly per NEC Table 250.66 based on the size of the largest ungrounded service-entrance conductor or equivalent area for parallel conductors.
- **Electrode Types:** NEC 250.52(A) defines permissible electrodes including 0.50 in (12.7 mm) diameter copper ground rods driven 8.0 ft (2.44 m) deep, concrete-encased electrodes (Ufer ground) of 20.0 ft (6.10 m) minimum length, and metal underground water pipes.

---

## 2. Bonding (Electrically Continuous Low-Impedance Path)

- **Definition Authority:** NEC Article 100; CEC Article 100.
- **Governing Article:** NEC Article 250, Part V and Part VII.
- **Physical Objective:** Joins non-current-carrying metallic parts of equipment and raceways together to establish an effective ground-fault current path.
- **Circuit Function:** Facilitates rapid operation of the overcurrent protective device (OCPD) under phase-to-ground fault conditions.
- **Equipment Grounding Conductor (EGC):** Sized strictly per NEC Table 250.122 based on the rating or setting of the upstream OCPD.
- **Main Bonding Jumper (MBJ):** Sized per NEC 250.28 and Table 250.102(C)(1).
